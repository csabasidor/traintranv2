## The four decisions

### 1. Country effects: fixed effects and clustering, and what each does
- **Country fixed effects** (`C(country)`, sample `country effects`) move the **point estimate**: does the relationship hold once each country's level is absorbed?
- **Clustered SEs** (sample `clustered`) move only the **interval**. They are applied where a model pools at least 5 countries; with that few clusters the cluster-robust asymptotics are thin, so they are a check beside HC3, not a replacement.
- **Domestic models** are one country, so they stay pooled: a country term cannot be identified.
- **Foreign transfer penalty Δα, route km:** pooled 30.6 min [15.9, 45.2]; country effects 45.6 min [36.7, 54.5]; clustered 30.6 min [7.3, 53.8]. Domestic pooled: 34.5 [28.3, 40.7].
- **Pooled correlations:** 2 pooled correlation(s) have a sign that differs from most of the within-country ones (Simpson's paradox); see `correlations.csv`.

### 2. Functional form: a primary form per model, by a fixed rule
- **The rule** (RESET level 0.01): linear passes → linear; linear rejects and log–log passes → log–log; both reject → the linear coefficient is reported as a descriptive average gradient, with a caveat.
- **Result:** 7 of 20 models keep linear, 6 switch to log–log, and 7 carry the caveat: domestic_one_transfer decay route, foreign_direct decay rail, foreign_one_transfer decay rail, foreign_one_transfer frequency rail, foreign_one_transfer joint rail, domestic transfer route, foreign transfer rail.
- **The audit** compares the submitted manuscript's Košice coefficients with this run; it applies to the Košice destination only, so it is not read here.

### 3. Influential observations: named, never dropped from the headline models

### 4. Spatial autocorrelation: what it means for the intervals
- **Size:** residual Moran's I is significant (p < 0.05, k = 5) in 24 of 24 headline fits, and significant at every k (3, 5, 8, 10) in 24.
- **Outcome vs residuals:** the outcome's I is 0.51–0.99 and the residuals' 0.32–0.96; the covariates absorb a median 16% of the outcome's spatial structure. The remainder is corridor structure: LAUs on one line share the same trains.
- **Spatial check (decay and joint models):** spatial-error coefficients against OLS: domestic_direct decay route_km_mean: OLS 0.7884 → 0.9249 (λ = 0.93); domestic_direct joint log_rail_km_mean: OLS -0.7196 → -0.6255 (λ = 0.51); domestic_direct joint log_population: OLS 0.3123 → 0.2660 (λ = 0.51); domestic_one_transfer decay route_km_mean: OLS 0.8008 → 0.7278 (λ = 0.90); domestic_one_transfer joint log_rail_km_mean: OLS -0.6243 → -0.6668 (λ = 0.73); domestic_one_transfer joint log_population: OLS 0.3725 → 0.3172 (λ = 0.73); foreign_direct decay route_km_mean: OLS 0.5562 → 0.6068 (λ = 0.91); foreign_direct joint log_rail_km_mean: OLS -1.2767 → -1.0294 (λ = 0.68); foreign_direct joint log_population: OLS 0.2361 → 0.1274 (λ = 0.68); foreign_one_transfer decay route_km_mean: OLS 0.6907 → 0.6467 (λ = 0.91); foreign_one_transfer joint log_rail_km_mean: OLS -1.1958 → -1.3913 (λ = 0.73); foreign_one_transfer joint log_population: OLS 0.3216 → 0.4240 (λ = 0.73).

- **Reading:** the OLS coefficients are reported as the estimates. Their HC3 intervals are **too narrow** where `si_understated` is true in `models_summary.csv`, and should be read as lower bounds on the uncertainty. Where the spatial-error coefficient differs from OLS, both are reported.
