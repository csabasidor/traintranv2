## The four decisions

### 1. Country effects: fixed effects and clustering, and what each does
- **Country fixed effects** (`C(country)`, sample `country effects`) move the **point estimate**: does the relationship hold once each country's level is absorbed?
- **Clustered SEs** (sample `clustered`) move only the **interval**. They are applied where a model pools at least 5 countries; with that few clusters the cluster-robust asymptotics are thin, so they are a check beside HC3, not a replacement.
- **Domestic models** are one country, so they stay pooled: a country term cannot be identified.
- **Foreign transfer penalty Δα, route km:** pooled 56.6 min [30.9, 82.3]; country effects 75.8 min [58.0, 93.7]; clustered 56.6 min [14.7, 98.4]. Domestic pooled: 63.1 [57.8, 68.3].
- **Pooled correlations:** 2 pooled correlation(s) have a sign that differs from most of the within-country ones (Simpson's paradox); see `correlations.csv`.

### 2. Functional form: a primary form per model, by a fixed rule
- **The rule** (RESET level 0.01): linear passes → linear; linear rejects and log–log passes → log–log; both reject → the linear coefficient is reported as a descriptive average gradient, with a caveat.
- **Result:** 11 of 20 models keep linear, 3 switch to log–log, and 6 carry the caveat: domestic_one_transfer decay route, domestic_one_transfer decay rail, domestic_one_transfer frequency rail, domestic transfer route, domestic transfer rail, foreign transfer route.
- **The audit, read against it:** 5 of the stated coefficients fall outside their interval. The specification behind 2 of them is rejected by RESET, while 0 of the claims within their interval also rest on a rejected form.

  | | form rejected | form not rejected |
  |---|---|---|
  | outside the interval | 2 | 3 |
  | within the interval | 0 | 10 |

  So the failures do **not** all sit where the form is rejected: the functional form explains only part of the audit failures.

### 3. Influential observations: named, never dropped from the headline models
- **Named:** 1,093 LAU × model rows with Cook's D > 4/n are listed in `influential_observations.csv`, 7 of them with D > 1: Bratislava (domestic_direct decay route, all, D = 1.05, 480,902 inh.), Trnava (domestic_direct decay route, pop < 100k, D = 1.22, 63,184 inh.), Trenčianska Teplá (domestic_direct decay route, excl. top-5 influential, D = 1.17, 4,131 inh.), Trnava (domestic_direct decay rail, pop < 100k, D = 1.10, 63,184 inh.), Ужгородська (foreign_direct decay route, country effects, D = 1.02, 115,449 inh.), Ужгородська (foreign_direct decay rail, country effects, D = 1.22, 115,449 inh.), Hidasnémeti (foreign_direct power route, time, D = 1.09, 950 inh.).
- **Large cities?** Of the 5 distinct LAUs with D > 1, 2 have 100,000 inhabitants or more, so population is only a partial proxy for influence; the top-5 refit removes the influential LAUs themselves.
- **Sensitivity:** the refit without the five most influential LAUs is reported beside every decay fit (`excl. top-5 influential`, `n_removed`).
- **The large-city refit (pop < 100k):** it removes domestic_direct route: 1, domestic_direct rail: 1, domestic_one_transfer route: 1, domestic_one_transfer rail: 1, foreign_direct route: 7, foreign_direct rail: 7, foreign_one_transfer route: 25, foreign_one_transfer rail: 25 LAUs. Domestically that is a single LAU, so it establishes nothing there.

### 4. Spatial autocorrelation: what it means for the intervals
- **Size:** residual Moran's I is significant (p < 0.05, k = 5) in 22 of 24 headline fits, and significant at every k (3, 5, 8, 10) in 19.
- **Outcome vs residuals:** the outcome's I is 0.49–0.96 and the residuals' 0.07–0.77; the covariates absorb a median 24% of the outcome's spatial structure. The remainder is corridor structure: LAUs on one line share the same trains.
- **Spatial check (decay and joint models):** spatial-error coefficients against OLS: domestic_direct decay route_km_mean: OLS 0.8234 → 0.8049 (λ = 0.52); domestic_direct joint log_rail_km_mean: OLS -0.3885 → -0.2724 (λ = 0.78); domestic_direct joint log_population: OLS 0.2335 → 0.1947 (λ = 0.78); domestic_one_transfer decay route_km_mean: OLS 0.7376 → 0.6970 (λ = 0.84); domestic_one_transfer joint log_rail_km_mean: OLS -0.5073 → -0.6167 (λ = 0.78); domestic_one_transfer joint log_population: OLS 0.3635 → 0.2568 (λ = 0.78); foreign_direct decay route_km_mean: OLS 0.7659 → 0.7600 (λ = 0.36); foreign_direct joint log_rail_km_mean: OLS -0.5308 → -0.5152 (λ = 0.60); foreign_direct joint log_population: OLS 0.1273 → 0.1320 (λ = 0.60); foreign_one_transfer decay route_km_mean: OLS 0.7632 → 0.6924 (λ = 0.81); foreign_one_transfer joint log_rail_km_mean: OLS -1.2729 → -1.4770 (λ = 0.71); foreign_one_transfer joint log_population: OLS 0.2041 → 0.3130 (λ = 0.71).
  The OLS coefficient lies inside the spatial-error interval in 8 of 12 cases.
- **Reading:** the OLS coefficients are reported as the estimates. Their HC3 intervals are **too narrow** where `si_understated` is true in `models_summary.csv`, and should be read as lower bounds on the uncertainty. Where the spatial-error coefficient differs from OLS, both are reported.
